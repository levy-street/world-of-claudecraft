# World of ClaudeCraft
## Character VFX design guides
Prepared for Tony | Review edition | 8 September 2026

Nine classes. Twenty-seven specializations. One shared standard of satisfaction.

This is the proposed creative direction before heavy implementation continues. It describes how the abilities should look, move, sound and feel, with enough detail to guide concept art, animation, effects and review together. Early Warrior experiments remain unfinished work and are not presented as approved designs.

The central promise is that every character should feel expertly made and exciting to play. A Rogue's precise killing stroke, a tank catching a crushing blow and a healer saving the group should each deliver a memorable payoff in their own language.

The guides use the current game's ability and specialization definitions, public combat reports, Riot's League of Legends VFX guide, all ten supplied Riot education episodes through their captions, and the additional studio references listed at the end. Proposed imagery is clearly distinguished from established game facts.

The color samples in this document are proposed art targets. They are not instructions to repaint character models. Each specialization inherits its class model-preservation palette and receives a separate, brighter VFX palette. These preservation colors are illustrative envelopes, not measured model pixels. The guides describe proposed composition and timing; they are not finished game artwork.

Review focus: the class fantasy, each specialization's identity, the relative weight of its abilities, and whether the proposed standout moments are exciting enough. Technical implementation remains paused for this review.

# The shared art direction
## Genre, style and tone
A heroic, stylized fantasy MMO with compact, readable characters and painterly environments. Effects should feel sculpted and hand-authored: clear silhouettes, rich material bodies, purposeful texture, and a small number of decisive bright accents. The tone can support brutal melee, sacred spectacle and playful conjuring without making them look like different games.

AAA quality here means deliberate design and excellent execution at the actual combat camera. It does not require photorealistic smoke, unrestricted glare or a screen covered in particles. The aim is much greater detail, scale and satisfaction through stronger composition and material behavior.

## What we take from League of Legends
Riot's guide places gameplay meaning, focal hierarchy and timing ahead of decoration. Its color section separates the model's palette from a brighter, wider VFX palette. Its texture and timing sections support painterly materials and clear anticipation, climax and dissipation. These are the shared design rules; the class concepts in this document are our proposed application. [R1, PDF pp. 6-21, 28-36]

## The five material questions
For every effect, specify its shape, edge softness, believability, motion and level of detail. A steel cut needs a sharp directional edge; its dust can be soft. Water folds and falls; electricity branches and snaps; living growth divides and unfurls. The chosen style must belong to the game. [R2]

## Three layers, one clear subject
Primary: the action and its actual recipient. Secondary: substantial material that gives the action scale and identity. Tertiary: droplets, chips, embers, fibers and fine glints that reward attention. Fine detail must survive close inspection without being necessary to understand the attack at a distance.

## Lore discipline
Use established class mechanics and the world's written lore as anchors. The game has the Dreamer, Smith and Gardener, and named orders, but those facts do not automatically make a playable class a member of an order. Any new visual symbolism below is an art proposal, not new canon. Foreign franchise symbols, deities and faction uniforms are not imported.

# Equal satisfaction across characters
## Equal care, different forms of power
Every specialization gets an excellent repeatable action, a distinctive build-up, a major payoff and a meaningful defensive or utility moment where its actual kit supports them. No class gets the leftovers after Warrior and Mage. Usage data changes the order and depth of work, not the minimum standard.

## A shared ladder within each kit
Everyday action: a confident pose, unmistakable contact or restoration, and a short satisfying sound. It should remain enjoyable after hundreds of uses.

Empowered action: the same recognizable ability gains a meaningful second structure, sharper timing or material transformation. The player should recognize that it is empowered without reading a number.

Spend or signature payoff: accumulated preparation visibly resolves. Add stronger body commitment, material mass, directional reach and a decisive ending. Preserve the true target footprint.

Major cooldown or rescue: a staged, memorable event, with clear activation and a quieter readable continuing state. A large group save earns as much care as an execution. Long cooldown alone does not justify falsely suggesting damage or invulnerability.

## Compare function with function
Compare common melee cuts with common bolts and common heals for responsiveness and finish. Compare finishers with large heals and major shield catches for payoff. Compare sustained channels with other channels for rhythm and readability. Do not compare their raw damage numbers or require identical screen coverage.

## How spectacle grows without flattening the kit
Increase the size of purposeful shapes, the range of motion, the richness of material breakup, the staging of the climax and the quality of sound. Keep the brightest area concentrated. Give a huge finisher a short, forceful peak and a substantial but receding material tail. Do not make ordinary attacks feel weak to manufacture contrast.

## The cross-class review
Show equal-length clips from all 27 specializations at matching camera distance and quality. Review responsiveness, recognizable fantasy, target feedback, build-up/payoff and repeat-cast cleanliness. Any specialization that feels less finished returns to production, even if its individual tests pass. The aim is equal satisfaction, not an identical number of particles.

# How combat reports inform the design
## Three different measurements
Recorded completed casts show how often a captured cast or channel finished; they do not count every activation, especially instantaneous or cancelled abilities. Effect events count individual hits, ticks, targets and triggered effects. Contribution share measures a portion of damage or effective healing within the sampled fight. These are different quantities and are labelled separately throughout the evidence appendix.

## What the evidence can support
The initial study used 45 selected character performances in current-build Nythraxis raid kills. It checked the reported totals and fight duration against the detailed responses. Additional searches cover missing specializations where public samples exist. These are selected reports, not a census of all players, and historical examples are labelled separately.

## How priorities are assigned
First attention goes to actions with frequent reliable use and a large contribution to the specialization's normal play. Signature mechanics, survival tools, interrupts, movement and crowd control also receive high priority when they define the character. Low damage share is not a reason to neglect a shield or group buff.

Red Harvest and Twinstrike justify deep work through both repeated contact and damage contribution. Arc Bolt and Skyfall deserve equally serious everyday polish because they dominate their sampled rotations. The high healing shares of Solemn Prayer and Wildmend mean these regular heals must feel as finished as a primary attack.

## Power is judged within the actual kit
We consider damage or healing per activation, resource investment, setup, cooldown, execution risk, number of true recipients, control value and defensive value. A frequent channel can generate many events without being the largest single action. A rare rescue can be critical even when absent from a raid damage ranking.

## Limits that remain visible
Unmeasured means unmeasured, not unused. Pet strikes and passive echoes remain attributed as the API reports them. Historical data can suggest what to inspect, but cannot establish current popularity or balance. The class concepts are creative decisions informed by this evidence, not statistical conclusions.

# The production and acceptance standard
## From intention to a finished ability
Record purpose, real recipients, cast rhythm, duration and the emotional promise. Gather focused material and movement references. Sketch alternate compositions, choose a clear silhouette, then block the body action and effect together. Test the result at gameplay distance before spending heavily on detail. Add textures, particles and sound only after the action works. [R3, Concept Art 01:26, 03:04, 06:57; Animation 05:15, 07:49]

## Timing and contact
Preparation makes the source legible. The climax coincides with the actual weapon, projectile, breath, healing or protection event. The ending makes room for the next action. A spammable instant must complete its prominent action within its real combat rhythm; a long cast or channel keeps its real duration. Never invent extra damaging hits to justify extra flashes.

## Characters and recipients
Preserve clothing, fur, skin, equipment and facing. Use brief local highlights, weapon glints and edge illumination. Wounds, frost, roots, curses and healing belong to the actual affected body. Preparation belongs to its actual owner: two players must not visually charge or consume one another's marks, wounds or pet readiness. Show missed, absorbed, cancelled and interrupted actions appropriately. Lasting states follow real duration, removal and consumption, including early breaks.

## Detail at every quality setting
The primary silhouette, direction, actual area and contact survive every supported setting. Higher settings add material depth, branching detail and richer debris. Reduced motion keeps the action understandable without camera shake or decorative drift. Busy fights should prioritize the meaningful contact over spare glitter. Technical preparation must avoid a first-use pause.

## Sound is part of the design
Use a distinct preparation, contact and tail for the material. Variation changes texture, not the timing of important cues. Common attacks need short tails and controlled repetition; larger actions get a stronger staged envelope. Heals, blocks and successful interrupts need positive, identifiable feedback. Distance and simultaneous sounds must not erase the local player's action. [R3, Sound Design 01:23, 04:59, 06:36, 09:45]

## Proof before acceptance
Compare matching before-and-after views and motion sequences. Test sustained spam, moving targets, several enemies, bright and dark terrain, all quality settings and relevant forms or equipment. Finish with two complete visual reviews across the entire catalogue. A beautiful isolated still and green code tests are both insufficient on their own.


# Warrior | Steel, will and physical consequence

Model preservation: `#687580`, `#624435`, `#783A3D`.


VFX family: `#25333B`, `#A50B24`, `#EE3345`, `#FFD0AC`.

## Fantasy and lore footing
The current specializations establish disciplined weapon craft, relentless dual-wield rage, and shield-led retaliation. The proposed common theme is a fighter whose trained body gives steel extraordinary force. Power begins in stance, hips, shoulders and equipment. It does not require an invented magical order or full-body color change.

## Shape and material language
Primary shapes are wedges, crossing cuts and shield faces. Secondary shapes are torn blood sheets, buckled metal fragments, pressure ridges and displaced grit. Tertiary detail includes directional sparks and gravity-led droplets. Steel has a hard bright edge over a darker substantial body; blood has dark red volume and irregular torn exits. Ground debris follows real ground contact.

## Movement and sound
Preparation compresses the stance. Contact drives through a visible target plane. Recovery carries momentum into a balanced stance or the next cut. Heavier moves gain body commitment and extraction, not an unexplained delay. Use steel rasp, body thud, plate resonance and a short tearing tail; distinguish dual-wield blades by their transients.

## Shared tools and states
Shouts need shaped outward pressure, breath and displaced dust across their real area, with readable response on recipients. Goad restores the above-head swearing animation; a short directed challenge ties it to the actual taunted enemy. Blood Toll is a brief self sacrifice and resource surge. Stances settle into a restrained weapon or guard accent. Enrage concentrates red around weapons and armor edges while the outfit stays recognizable.

## Rules and review priorities
Single-target finishers must not imply surrounding damage. Armor Shear weakens armor without dealing a hit. Jawcrack interrupts without inventing injury. Heroic Leap lands where the player actually arrives. Bloodhook belongs to Hunter and gets a physical hook there. All Warrior attacks must work with their allowed equipment, including credible single-weapon return strokes where relevant.

Reference application: directional receiving effects and authored intensity from Blizzard; body commitment and impact feedback from the cited combat discussion. The compositions below are proposed for this game. [R4, R6]

# Warrior | Battlecraft

VFX palette: `#25333B`, `#8A242C`, `#C18B54`, `#FFE2B3`.

## Identity: deliberate, expert brutality
Battlecraft reads as controlled leverage and weak-point exploitation. Its silhouette is one broad blade plane and a strongly committed shoulder line. Warm steel, dark iron and restrained wound red distinguish it from Bloodrush's crimson torrent. Long, weighty strokes still have crisp playable recovery.

## Relative power and priorities
Brute Swing establishes the dependable everyday hit. Redhand is a freely usable two-charge reversal attack that prepares Maiming Strike; it does not require a successful defence. Maiming Strike is the recurring signature wound; Breachmaker opens a vulnerability window. Early Grave is the execution payoff. Widening Arc amplifies eligible strikes onto real extra victims. Historical combat samples, if cited in the appendix, do not establish current popularity.

## Signature designs
Maiming Strike: plant the lead foot, draw the shoulder back, then drive a broad oblique cut into the torso. A brief ivory seam appears on the actual contact plane; dark wound material opens behind it and falls away. The continuing Gaping Wounds state pulses locally on real bleed ticks. The healing reduction receives a restrained local wound accent rather than a second full attack.

Redhand and Breachmaker: Redhand uses a compact draw-and-return action with a clean reversal of steel, without inventing a blocked incoming blow. Its two possible Maiming Strike empowerment stages gain distinct restrained weapon detail, then visibly resolve when consumed. Breachmaker drives a heavier wedge through a guard, followed by separated plate or guard fragments and a persistent source-owned weakness mark. Their silhouettes and endings must differ from Maiming Strike.

Early Grave: a compressed preparation and decisive execution line, with the strongest extraction and low contact in this rotation. It should feel dangerous even against a large target. Its large material wake stays directional; no circular explosion suggests an invented area attack.

## Empowerment and defence
Sudden Death briefly catches the blade edge with a sharp readiness accent, then clears on use or expiry. Die by the Sword shows active defensive blade planes and successful deflections. Avatar needs a monumental activation and continuing sense of weight, with the real armor still visible. Reaping Arc follows actual recipients, including the current all-around mechanics until any gameplay discrepancy is separately resolved.

## Finish check
With color removed, a viewer should distinguish the reversal, recurring heavy cut and execution from their line of action. The execution feels heavier, but Brute Swing still feels professionally finished and satisfying.

# Warrior | Bloodrush

VFX palette: `#330D18`, `#A50B24`, `#EE3345`, `#FFD0AC`.

## Identity: rage with a readable rhythm
Bloodrush is relentless, embodied aggression. Alternating blade planes, torn scarlet material and a forward-driving stance make its identity unmistakable. More bleed and red are essential to this redesign. A narrow hot edge gives each contact clarity while darker blood supplies scale.

## Relative power and evidence
In the matched raid samples, Red Harvest contributes 25.4-28.6% of damage and Twinstrike 20.1-21.1%; their recorded effect rates are roughly 32-33 and 35 per minute respectively. These are component events, not cast counts. Both deserve flagship quality. Bloodletting remains a satisfying sustain attack; Red Harvest carries the larger rage payoff.

## Twinstrike: two crossing commitments
A descending entry tears open the guard; a differently posed return stroke crosses it. Show two distinct body turns and hand trajectories, with the correct weapon contact. Each cut has a broad scarlet-backed trail, a brief ivory seam, directional steel fragments and blood that visibly starts on the actual victim. The second cut has a stronger reversal. Target wound angles change with the blade plane.

Proposed visual contacts are 0.15 and 0.34 seconds, with recovery by 0.66 seconds. The two real hit results remain independent: a missed cut keeps its movement but cannot paint blood. Full absorption receives a protective contact, not a flesh wound.

## Red Harvest: the flagship finisher
Coil, open the guard, reverse, then pull the final cut through in a huge ascending extraction. The third beat has the broadest torn-blood fan, strongest low contact, aggressive recoil and richest fragment breakup. Proposed contacts are 0.15, 0.32 and 0.49 seconds; the prominent tail clears by 0.72 seconds. Scale comes from a composed fan and forceful movement, never repeated expanding white rings.

## The surrounding kit
Bloodletting has one actual weapon hit plus visible self restoration; any recovery motion must not become a second wound. Bladed Gyre has a real surrounding attack and a legible follow-up empowerment. Mayhem/Enrage keeps red along weapon wakes and edges for its real four-second state. Emboldening Roar sends a substantial rallying pressure field to eligible allies and leaves a concise readiness cue. Furious Mending gains a readable ten-second defensive state; actual Bloodletting hits trigger its much larger self-restoration. Use a forceful inward recovery at those hits, with no autonomous healing stream or whole-model repaint.

## Finish check
Twinstrike must feel excellent at sustained rotation speed. Red Harvest must remain unmistakably larger and more violent. Their opening contacts cannot be interchangeable short red bands.

# Warrior | Ironguard

VFX palette: `#273843`, `#738A91`, `#C49A62`, `#F5E2BD`.

## Identity: a frontline that answers force
Ironguard uses a shield as a load-bearing object. Flattened planes, displaced metal and low compression give it a distinct silhouette. The proposed palette is cool iron with warm impact accents; blood is incidental rather than the dominant material. A successful defence should feel as satisfying as a successful strike.

## Relative power and evidence
Shieldcrack contributes 21.2-24.8% of damage in the matched samples and is the central recurring contact to perfect. Revenge is a directional retaliatory sweep. Quaking Blow establishes area pressure and slow. Faultline is a larger forward control event. Raised Guard and Iron Resolve are essential satisfaction moments regardless of their absence from damage-share rankings.

## Shieldcrack and Revenge
Shieldcrack: draw the shield tight, drive its boss through the target plane, briefly compress the recipient and release a broad, short metallic fracture fan. Detail begins at the real shield face. The sound combines a dense body impact with a bounded plate ring. Recovery returns the shield to a believable guard.

Revenge: a braced reversal sweeps through the actual forward arc, with an asymmetric iron wake and separate receiving hits on each struck enemy. Avoid a generic full-circle spin. A free-use proc adds a restrained fracture-light cue to the shield, then resolves into the strike.

## Faultline and Quaking Blow
Faultline sends a branching, heavy ground rupture into the real forward area; stone lifts progressively along its direction and the stun remains readable on affected enemies. Quaking Blow is a more compact compression: low ridges, grit and a short pressure front. It still gets excellent ground contact, without stealing Faultline's large directional identity.

## Defensive and threat feedback
Raised Guard sets overlapping shield-edge braces and answers incoming force with localized deflection. Iron Resolve forms a dense reinforcing layer that visibly spends at incoming contact and ends on depletion or expiry. Direhowl and Defiant Bellow use different vocal shapes: oppressive low pressure versus a confrontational challenge. Goad's overhead swearing is readable and tied to the actual mob forced to attack.

## Finish check
A tank comparison clip must include blocking and absorbing under pressure, not just damage abilities. The shield catch, counterattack and emergency reinforcement should each provide a distinct, forceful reward.


# Rogue | Precision, deception and dirty fighting

Model preservation: `#292B35`, `#5B4944`, `#6C6265`.


VFX family: `#181825`, `#B2C8CB`, `#A01430`, `#90BF24`, `#593476`.

## Fantasy and lore footing
The kit supports a resourceful close fighter, with distinct routes through venom, brawling and deception. Proposed details draw on honed blades, leather, dirty cloth, viscous coatings and displaced shadow. No invented assassin order is required. Compact effects can be lavishly authored and extremely satisfying without turning the Rogue into a heavy armored fighter.

## Shape and material language
Primary shapes are needle lines, serrated wedges, hooked cuts and broken silhouettes. Secondary shapes are local wound ribbons, thrown granular matter, cloth-like shadow and poisonous liquid. Tertiary detail includes metal grit, individual dirt grains and droplets. The important edge is quick and precise; supporting material gives it enough body to read at combat distance.

## Movement and sound
Use asymmetric shoulders, weight transfers, short deceptive preparations and fast recoveries. A puncture drives forward; a slash pulls across; a brawler commits hip and fist. Sounds separate dry steel entry, wet wound detail, cloth motion and gritty body impact. Avoid turning every attack into the same bright crossed slash.

## Shared utility deserves authored actions
Dirt Toss visibly scoops or throws granular dirt into the victim's face. Its incapacitation has a sustained readable state, but the throw causes no damage and should not draw a blood wound. Boot interrupts at close range. Shadeslip has clear departure and arrival without inventing damage. Smoke Screen grants dodge, not stealth; Smokestep and Duskveil must remain distinguishable concealment actions.

## The ordinary finisher: Dirt Nap
Brace low, draw the blade across a clear vulnerable plane and finish with an emphatic extraction, a localized wound fan and a dry body impact. Actual combo investment increases commitment, material mass and the final sound. This base finisher must feel excellent on ordinary cycles; Venomrend exceeds it through prepared wound consumption, and Lights Out through a distinctly heavier body blow.

## Coatings and control
Weapon coatings begin on the weapon and add local receiving material only on actual procs. Festering Venom currently behaves as a weapon imbue, so its name is not permission to invent stacking periodic poison. Low Blow, Sap and Armor Breach need visible purpose without a false damage flash. Control ends on the actual break or expiry.

## Finish check
The Rogue's fast cadence must remain clean within its actual one-second base GCD and haste limits. Every core action should still have a satisfying contact, with finishers gaining commitment and precise payoff rather than lingering screen clutter.

# Rogue | Knifework

VFX palette: `#24232A`, `#A01430`, `#789B20`, `#C1DD56`, `#D9EBCE`.

## Identity: a lethal incision and a prepared poison
Knifework feels exact, intimate and dangerous. Thin pale knife edges sit over dark wounds and thick green venom. The poison is a liquid that beads, clings and eats into a local mark; it is not green fire. A ritual builds through actual player state and resolves in a decisive wound payoff.

## Relative power and evidence
Craven Thrust contributes 27.9% of damage in the single matched raid sample; Venomrend contributes 10.2%, with its triggered components counted separately from casts. Prioritize the everyday rear attack and the transformed finisher together. Venom Dart establishes ranged preparation. Bleed Out and Throat Wire supply wounds that later pay off. Killer's Calm is readiness, not an attack by itself.

## Craven Thrust
Set a clear rear-facing line, draw the dagger close to the ribs and drive a narrow puncture into the actual back. Give the entry a concentrated silver bite, a dark spreading seam and a short angled extraction. The posture should look like a controlled thrust from behind, not a broad Warrior swing. A small pull of blood follows the blade out.

## Venomrend
At the real six-stage Ritual threshold, the finisher changes visibly: a compact ritual binding gathers near the weapon, without permanent UI-like numbers above the character. On use, the blade enters once, existing wound threads tighten and collapse into the target, and a new viscous venom wound takes their place. The consumption is the dramatic climax; periodic ticks thereafter stay local and do not replay the finisher.

## Supporting ability distinctions
Venom Dart has a visible hand or weapon release, a narrow dart and a sticky puncture. Throat Wire visibly tightens a fine wire at the neck, with pressure and a continuing bleed rather than a floating ring. Bleed Out opens a longer wound with repeated restrained tick response. Melting Acid pits armor locally; Nightshade Coating emphasizes a sickly impairment mark. Both must retain their actual direct-hit and debuff behavior.

## State, sound and review
Killer's Calm places one cold glint along the blade, consumed by the empowered attack or expiry. Ritual preparation uses fine glassy liquid sounds; the finisher combines a sharp entry, wet constriction and short corrosive decay. The payoff must feel substantial at normal camera distance while retaining this spec's surgical identity.

# Rogue | Thuggery

VFX palette: `#272129`, `#8F3332`, `#C59662`, `#E1C49B`, `#F7E6C5`.

## Identity: a dangerous scrapper
Thuggery's fantasy is physical escalation: rough cuts become committed body blows. Chipped steel, leather motion, grit and bruising red make the palette warmer and more grounded than Knifework. Shapes are short serrated wedges and compressed impact masses, with strongly asymmetrical poses.

## Relative power and evidence
Wicked Slash contributes 18.4-35.3% of damage in the matched samples and deserves exceptional everyday polish. Haymaker and Lights Out are less frequent but carry the Redline escalation. Dirt Nap remains the ordinary finisher. Mirrored Blades increases attack speed in its base effect; its name does not justify inventing area hits.

## Wicked Slash and Haymaker
Wicked Slash is a short, vicious draw-cut with a visible shoulder lead, a chipped ivory edge and directional wound material. It is compact but never a token spark. Its rhythm should make repeated casting satisfying without a repeated full-body spin.

As the actual Redline state builds, Haymaker changes the action into a committed fist-and-hip blow. A dense local compression, cloth and grit displacement, and a low body impact establish the difference. The glove or fist is the source; the target's body is the receiving surface. Avoid blood-blade imagery that would make the punch read as another knife cut.

## Lights Out
The finisher visibly closes Redline. A short lower-body brace drives an emphatic final blow, with stronger target recoil, a broad asymmetric grit fan and a heavy short sound. The animation must resolve the stored tension, not merely recolor Dirt Nap. Material scale increases while the target remains visible. A high-value contact followed by falling grit clears promptly for the next action.

## Buffs and group identity
Quickened Blood is an instant energy restoration, so it gets a crisp inward pulse and exhale rather than an invented long frenzy. Cutthroat Tempo accents the caster's rhythm and weapons despite its target requirement. Thieves' Chorus deserves an energetic group cue: sharp cloth-like pressure strokes and brief responding accents on actual allies, with a shared rhythmic sound and a subdued continuing state.

## Finish check
At equal camera distance, a player should distinguish blade entry, body blow and Redline finisher from pose and material alone. The last blow must deliver a payoff comparable in satisfaction to the other classes' spenders, without mimicking their magic.

# Rogue | Skulduggery

VFX palette: `#151526`, `#473458`, `#86659B`, `#A8304F`, `#D7D0E7`.

## Identity: the strike you recognized too late
Skulduggery uses concealment, false positioning and a precise moment of exposure. Proposed shadow behaves like thin displaced cloth and missing silhouette fragments. The primary attack remains a real blade and body; echoes are subordinate fragments of its motion, never a second equally bright character competing for attention.

## Relative power and evidence
Lurker's Strike contributes 24.6-27.8% of damage in the matched samples. Red Ribbon contributes 7.8-11.4% and supplies a recurring wound identity. Gloam builds toward Shadow Veil; the first empowered opener is the payoff. Bleed Out and Throat Wire remain local continuing wounds. Echo contribution is recorded separately and must not be mistaken for extra casts.

## Lurker's Strike
Use a compressed hidden preparation and an abrupt, strongly directed blade entry from the valid attack position. A displaced shadow sliver separates from the shoulder a moment before contact, then folds into the primary cut. The receiving wound is sharply placed and briefly brighter than the fading shadow. Do not hide the contact behind a large opaque smoke ball.

## Red Ribbon and Shadow Veil
Red Ribbon draws a thin but substantial crimson wound filament through the actual cut; its continuing bleed and vulnerability stay attached to the victim. This is a visible material motif, not an orbit around the caster.

Three-stage Gloam readiness gathers fine silhouette slivers near the weapon. The prepared opener itself spends all three Gloam and starts Shadow Veil's six-second combat window, permitting openers without stealth or rear positioning. There is no separate activation cast and this is not concealment. Distinguish the triggering strike from the first doubled Lurker's Strike if that occurs later: its larger negative-space cut and decisive wound release spend the first-hit reward. Later permitted openers retain their true state without repeating that reward.

## Movement and protection
Shadeslip leaves a thin departure tear and a precise arrival, preserving the real destination and no-damage behavior. Wraith Strike couples a genuine weapon contact to a brief defensive afterimage. Shadecloak forms a close shadow mantle with visible force meeting its boundary and ends on absorb depletion or expiry. Keep silhouettes readable against dark environments with a controlled pale rim.

## Sound and finish check
Use short air suction, cloth pull, a very clear primary cut and a quieter delayed echo. The first opener must feel powerful even without color or bloom. Echoes should enrich its timing without obscuring who attacked or suggesting a second full-strength strike.


# Hunter | A skilled shot and a living companion

Model preservation: `#62513F`, `#4B6145`, `#8A7352`.


VFX family: `#79512E`, `#D09043`, `#A9D7DC`, `#77CDDD`, `#9C5A3D`.

## Fantasy and lore footing
Hunter's identity comes from marksmanship, a real pet partnership and practical field tools. Its effects should show how an action works: cord tension, arrow release, hook engagement, trap closure, claws and jaws. The class can be spectacular without making every shot into a spell or replacing beasts with unrelated humanoid apparitions.

## Shape, materials and palette
Primary shapes are straight shot lines, ballistic arcs, articulated mechanisms and animal silhouettes. Secondary shapes are fletching wakes, splinter fans, taut cable and compressed dirt. Tertiary detail includes feather fibers, dust, chipped metal and small frost crystals. Preserve leather, cloth and natural pet fur; flashes and edge highlights connect the action to the actor.

## The shared weapon language
Hands must draw and release the equipped weapon convincingly. Projectiles point where they travel, including in a still frame. Long Draw never repaints the whole character. Fevered Draw is a deliberate return to the rapid staccato rhythm Tony preferred, upgraded in animation, material and sound. Volley keeps its impressive scale while adding upward aim and correctly oriented descending arrows.

## Utility and companion care
Wildbond establishes a visible directed connection with the actual beast. Patch Up distinguishes living-pet healing from revival. Release Companion ends that relationship cleanly. Shellskin protects and prevents attacks, so its posture and defensive material must explain the limitation. Guise and rally states are readable accents, not a zoo of arbitrary animal ghosts around the Hunter.

## Distinct impacts
Fell Shot has a controlled magical shot core; Venom Barb deposits a local lingering wound; Rattling Shot carries a blunt, disruptive receiving contact. Hushing Shot breaks the actual cast without inventing a damage explosion. Fieldcraft tools show real hardware and terrain interaction. Pet-origin damage is visibly performed by the pet.

## Finish check
Compare bow action, projectile direction and target reaction as one event. Review different pets, moving channels, close targets and group fights. High detail must strengthen this practical identity rather than bury it under colored mist. [R1; R5, direction and silhouette]

# Hunter | Coldsight

VFX palette: `#284552`, `#668A99`, `#A9D7DC`, `#DCEBF0`, `#CBAB70`.

## Identity: absolute aim, then relentless release
Coldsight is composed precision. Narrow cool metal, taut lines and fletching motion give its shots structure. The cold palette suggests control; it does not mean every projectile freezes enemies. Distinguish the long committed shot from the rapid moving channel through posture, spacing, audio and projectile mass.

## Relative power and evidence
Current-build dungeon samples now cover this spec. Across two samples totalling 157.65 seconds of recorded fight time, Fevered Draw has six recorded completions and 38 damage effect events: 2.28 completions per minute and 14.46 effect events per minute. One cancelled channel is also recorded, so those events cannot all be attributed to the six completions. It illustrates why hits cannot be labelled casts. Long Draw, Measured Shot and the channel all deserve direct review; the appendix records the limited sample scope.

## Long Draw
Set the feet, pull the bow visibly to full tension and compress the motion near release. Small highlights converge along the held weapon; a brief edge flash releases into a substantial, tightly directed arrow wake. The target receives a strong puncture and directional material exit. The character's normal colors remain intact. The effect must feel powerful because of tension and release, not a prolonged whole-body glow.

## Fevered Draw
Revisit the older Uzi-like version as a motion reference, then author a disciplined rapid-fire performance. Six actual shots over the current 2.4-second moving channel get six readable releases, each with a distinct short string transient, arrow orientation and receiving contact. Mage fire is controlled volatile combustion; Ruination fire has siege mass and darker crust; Shaman fire rides the actual elemental weapon or shock. A common flame texture is not sufficient to define all three.

Paladin light has physical oath and equipment structure. Doctrine constructs wards and transfer links. Benison opens great prayers and guardian wings. Their warm palettes need different silhouettes and timing.

Vespers binds Effigy-linked suffering. Hexcraft positions an Eye and resolves condemnation. Necromancy commands actual undead and extracts souls. None should become generic purple smoke with arbitrary ghost creatures.

## Six healing identities, each with an earned climax
Sunmender: directed warmth, worked light and a completed sanctuary. Doctrine: ordered protection, enemy contact and eligible restorative transfer. Benison: immediate generous prayer and guardian intervention. Chronomancy: prepared echoes and visibly reversed injury. Spiritmend: stored liquid currents released through actual recipients. Groveheart: planted living growth, a distributed harvest and fresh planting.

Review these identities side by side. The list is about visual purpose, not a claim that every spell in each kit fits only one shape.

## Defensive power must be visible
A block needs a convincing catch at the real shield or protective surface. An absorb needs an armed state, a receiving response, visible spending and a clean end. A dodge needs actual avoidance language. A rescue proc needs a distinct readiness state and a much stronger real intervention. These deserve the same authored detail as attacks.

## Spec augmentation has substance
An empowered ability must change something meaningful: blade plane, material structure, charge release, prepared recipient state or sound envelope. A hue swap alone does not explain a specialization. At the same time, augmentation must preserve the original ability's recognizable core and true gameplay effect.

# The per-ability production card
## Required before detailed construction
Name the class, specialization and exact live ability. State its emotional promise in one plain sentence. Record what it actually does, who it affects, how it is triggered, its cast and recovery rhythm, duration and whether a proc or form changes it. Separate the player's activation from damage ticks, echoes and continuing state.

## The visual specification
Choose the primary silhouette and source attachment. Define the secondary material body, tertiary detail, edge softness and travel direction. Select the inherited model-preservation palette and the separate VFX colors. Specify where the brightest value sits and how the material remains readable without bloom. Record the actual area boundary, if one exists.

## The performance specification
Write preparation, release, travel, contact, follow-through and end as a short sequence. Identify the real weapon, mouth, paw, shield or hand that leads it. Specify target response and the exact moment of sound arrival. Define how it changes for a major proc, lower health, accumulated resource, alternate target disposition or different form.

## The failure and repetition specification
Describe misses, full absorption, interrupted casts, cancelled channels, lost targets, early control breaks and expired buffs. Define what persists and what clears. Test repeated casting at the actual allowed rate, not an artificially slow demonstration. Preserve the main contact and true boundary when quality or crowd budgets reduce detail.

## The review package
Provide matched before-and-after frames and motion sequences with the same ability, form, equipment, camera, background and quality. Include a close view for material finish and a gameplay view for clarity. A separate crowded clip must show that the primary read survives. Record the intended feeling, observed shortfall and required revision in plain English.

## How these guides become work
Once the creative direction is reviewed, every ability receives this card using its class and spec guide. Frequent and high-contribution actions receive the deepest early work; utility and defensive identity remain fully covered. Complete Warrior, then Rogue, then the other kits, with independent full-catalogue review cycles at the end. Existing early code remains a work in progress, not an approved art baseline.
