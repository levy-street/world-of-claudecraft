# Warrior impact crescendo

The next pass enlarges the receiving burst substantially while preserving the
small-scale material detail established by the precision pass. It keeps the
authoritative collision, Red Harvest's opening slashes and delayed damage,
animation holds, recoil, audio and spell lifetimes unchanged.

`WARRIOR_IMPACT_REACH` expands the prepared carrier and authored ejection reach.
The shader compensates its coordinates by the same factor, so the contact seed,
fragment widths, pigment grain and gravity are not enlarged with it. Steel has
unequal cutting tongues with fractured faces and thin silver lips. Their main
faces clear before the independently travelling chips. Blood retains dark
pigment and broken wet highlights; its small particle wakes are dark material,
not luminous red needles. Lightning forks retain their attached root geometry.
Crush keeps the existing original Blender fragment sprite beneath its wider
pressure shoulders. No new pool, texture, light, scene object or simulation work.

The larger footprint makes subpixel filtering particularly important. The old
normalized-distance smoothing could lengthen thin chips and blood droplets well
beyond their intended endpoints. Both now integrate pixel coverage in distance
units with finite longitudinal support. The real WebGL regression is
`node scripts/warrior_impact_raster_probe.mjs tmp/fresh-raster-proof.json` with the
dev server running. It checks visibility and finite extent at different pixel
densities and orientations. The ordinary-contact reuse and reduced-motion
contracts remain covered by `tests/warrior_flash.test.ts`.

Review matching gameplay-distance casts, all graphics settings, bright terrain,
reduced motion and continuous multi-target combat. Increased coverage and shader
particle work have a real fragment cost despite reusing the same prepared pools;
record performance rather than claiming it is free. Retain rejected prototypes
and their source hashes. The existing broader gate and intermittent frame stalls
remain open until separately resolved. A published art-review checkpoint is not
final AAA approval or whole-game release certification.
