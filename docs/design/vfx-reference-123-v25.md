# Illustrated VFX targets, v25

Tony supplied the first batch on 8 September 2026 and will add more. These are
close visual targets, supplementing the approved character guides and Riot
process checklist. They supersede looser prototype material choices where the
prototype does not resemble the target. This is an intake and implementation
brief, not a claim that the pictured work has already shipped.

The originals remain in Desktop/123 (the redirected OneDrive Desktop). A dated
copy and SHA256 inventory are retained in studio-contact-pass/tony-references-123
and tony-reference-123-inventory.json. Image (7) duplicates image (2) exactly.
Do not turn the presentation boards into runtime textures with their backgrounds,
labels or panel lines. Reconstruct their effects as layered game assets.

## Shared visual rules

- Preserve each reference's main silhouette, material, colour balance and detail.
  Judge the match with the character visible at gameplay distance, not only a
  cropped close-up. Reference camera framing alone is not an ability range.
- The effects have tangible material: steel has layered bevels, poison hangs in
  viscous drops, ice fractures in planes, parchment has fibres and tears, and
  water carries highlights and bubbles. Coloured light alone is insufficient.
- Keep the leading edge and impact brightest. Separate a readable main shape,
  supporting motion, and small debris. Fine detail must not erase the silhouette.
- Build anticipation, sharp contact and material-specific breakup. A still image
  suggests motion but does not prove timing; review actual clips and repeated casts.
- Keep or increase the existing spectacle. Redistribute excessive overlap into
  readable depth and surrounding detail rather than reducing the effect's ambition.
- A finisher may use the board's full eruption vocabulary. A frequent attack needs
  the same craftsmanship inside its shorter recovery, with one real contact per
  actual hit. Decorative debris must not imply extra damage events.
- Class and spec identity must survive grey backgrounds, bright terrain, low
  settings and reduced motion. The character model retains its base palette.

## Warrior, image (10)

Battlecraft: layered silver steel with copper edges and restrained cold-blue
secondary motion. The crescent is thick, bevelled and directional, with a clear
cutting tip. Maiming Strike needs a committed diagonal cleft; Early Grave needs a
centred overhead sentence and a substantially heavier contact. They cannot share
one execution pose. Use sculpted mesh wakes, a brushed-metal/fissure texture,
short sprite impact flashes, metal splinters and a grounded dust response.

Bloodrush: paired hooked crimson blades, torn red surfaces, glowing narrow
cutting seams and scattered dark red fragments. Twinstrike's two contacts must
read separately, with asymmetric first and second wakes. Red Harvest culminates
in the board's violent upward crimson eruption and larger crossing clefts. Retain
blood on actual recipients and sharp white-red contact cores without turning the
whole effect orange or treating rage as generic fire. Use curved mesh backs,
animated torn-edge sprite detail, red droplets and larger timed finishing shards.

Ironguard: heavy interlocking steel shield segments, dark metal faces, bright
bevels and compact blue-white compression at contact. Shieldcrack starts with a
real planted shield drive, then a dense faceted collision and outward grit.
Defensive effects assemble recognisable shield architecture; they are not nested
plain domes. Preserve real defensive/taunt mechanics and do not invent damage.

Current shout fan/dust work is development infrastructure and a scale experiment.
It is not accepted as the final appearance of seven distinct shouts. The new
board requires clearer martial material, silhouettes and recipient feedback.

## Rogue, image (6)

Knifework: acid-green poison with suspended thick droplets against a dark steel
knife. Preserve the narrow lethal edge, liquid meniscus and stretched strings.
Use masked blade meshes and short liquid sprite sheets with ballistic droplets;
the poison imprint belongs on the actual victim.

Thuggery: fractured brass knuckles, compressed dusty fists and angular stone
splinters. Impacts need weight and a dense short flash. Dirt Toss must eject real
dirt particles and a granular sprite cloud, not a magical circle.

Skulduggery: deep violet-black smoke like torn cloth, sharp dagger silhouettes
and tapered dark wakes. Use layered alpha sprites and textured curved ribbons
with bright violet tips and sparse dark fragments. Preserve body readability.

## Mage, image (5)

Chronomancy: translucent violet glass, suspended golden grains and incomplete,
precise clock geometry. Use faceted glass meshes, particles with deliberate
suspension and short bright timing accents. Keep Temporal Barrier's unnecessary
inner animation removed. Clock marks are a theme, not a default for every spell.

Pyromancy: broad curling flame sheets with hot yellow-white leading cores,
orange folds and dark-red breakup. Combine directional mesh flame tongues,
animated flame sprites, embers and smoke. Dragonsbreath needs an actual breathing
pose and continuous mouth-to-target connection.

Cryomancy: sharp blue crystalline planes, fractured shards and snowflake-like
branching. Combine ice meshes, frost sprite blooms and snow particles. Frost
breath must come from the mouth; Rimelance does not require an overhead twirl.

## Druid, image (3)

Moongrove: translucent veined leaves, cool lunar crescents and small warm-gold
counterpoints. Use leaf/crescent meshes, veined alpha textures and petal motes.
Wildfang: torn bark, wet crimson cuts and forceful animal weight. New wolf form
must be recognisable and properly animated; the board's red-and-ivory muscle-like
wake is a material target, not permission for unrelated summoned animals.
Groveheart: veined teal-green leaves, dew, warm gold sap and opening flower cups.
Healing should unfurl with layered leaf sprites/meshes and separate droplets.
Roots remain visible for the actual stun duration. Wildward, Fleet Form and
Primal Reflexes retain the requested removal of extraneous animal apparitions.

## Hunter, image (2), duplicate image (7)

Packlord: heavy animal paw marks, pale claw shapes and an amber-connected pet
charge over earthy debris. Stampede copies must be the actual pet near its melee
victim, with deterministic gameplay changes and tests where necessary.
Coldsight: feathered steel arrows, needle-bright cool-blue cores and sharp paired
reticle wedges. Long Draw uses a brief edge highlight, not a body recolour.
Fieldcraft: rough rope, bone barbs, dirty green poison and physical trip-lines.
Traps and projectiles need recognisable objects and ground contact. Volley keeps
its scale with arrows descending point-first after the hunter aims skyward.

## Shaman, image (8)

Thundercall: blue-white branching lightning split through dark fractured stone,
with restrained gold branches and expelled chips. Warspirit: ivory-and-blue
wind streams, rune-cut standing stones and orbiting fragments with weight.
Spiritmend: clear teal water, pearl-like cores, bubbles and shallow caustics.
Use separate lightning ribbons, rigid rune meshes, flowing water meshes and
sprite splashes. Their materials and motion must remain visibly different.

## Priest, image (9)

Doctrine: torn pale parchment with gold/blue writing and translucent geometric
pages. Shields and moving verses should assemble as pages, not generic bubbles.
Benison: candle-warm ivory, fine golden fibres, pearl-like motes and curved
sanctuary wings. Vespers: dense violet-black ink, eclipsed negative space, torn
shards and a sharp star-shaped climax. Use paper meshes, fibre sprites and ink
flipbooks separately, with clear values against both dark and light backgrounds.

## Warlock, image (4)

Hexcraft: viscous purple resin, knotted fibres, sharp occult spokes and taut
threads. Necromancy: porous weathered bone, hollow cavities, pale green souls
and irregular rib structures. Ruination: fractured black volcanic plates with
orange fissures and heavy burning shards. Use solid sculpted materials plus
appropriate liquid/soul/fire sprites. Preserve a distinct impact silhouette
for each ability rather than recolouring one explosion across the three specs.

## Paladin, image (11)

Sunmender: liquid gold, chalice-like petal cups, warm light ribbons and rising
healing drops. Faithwarden: ivory/gold architectural shield panels with clear
edges and a sun core. Dawnreaver: a sharp golden sword-like core through dark
rock, followed by angular sunlit fragments. Build layered shapes with polished
material detail; Ward of Faith must not gain an irrelevant nested inner barrier.

## Sprite timing sheets

Image (1): sixteen cells show a small ember, growing charge, sharp starburst,
three-lobed rolling flame climax, then darkening broken smoke and isolated embers.
Use that progression when authoring an appropriate fire impact: do not simply
scale a static fireball. Preserve the distinct flash and the dark breakup phase.

Image without a number: sixteen cells show a fine golden seed/spark developing
into curved petal lines, a radiant centre and a chalice/flower shape, then
individual pieces fading at different rates. This supports Sunmender/Benison
healing motifs. Rebuild clean alpha layers and use the dark backing only where
it belongs to the effect; the sheet's pale background is not runtime content.

Additional files will be inventoried and reviewed as Tony supplies them. The

segmented shield impacts, while the existing preparation fixes are verified.
