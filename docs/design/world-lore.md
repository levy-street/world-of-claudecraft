# World Lore Bible: The Long Waking

A unified mythology for every map area. It is built to *explain* what is already
in the game rather than sit beside it: why the dead won't rest (the whole
Gravecaller campaign), why the Veiled Hollow is reachable only by portal, why
the Amberfall's leaves never fall, why no one tends the Evergarden, why the
Galecrest's wind never stops, why Rifts tear open and are never the same twice,
and why the world keeps growing northward, release after release.

Tone: cozy-dark English mythic. Gods have titles, not names: their true names
drowned with the civilization that knew them (see: the Night of Glass).

---

## 1. The Three

Before the world, there were three siblings. Mortals remember only their
offices:

- **The Dreamer**: eldest. She did not build the world; she *dreams it, ahead
  of itself*. Everything that exists was first a thread in her dream, and the
  world's northern edge is simply where the dreaming has gotten to. She hung
  her own heart in the sky as a star, **the Loom**, to hold the dream steady
  while she worked, and she began weaving a second, kinder world to hang beside
  this one. She also kept the dead: to die was to be gathered back into her
  dream, which is why every old tongue calls death *the Sleep*.
- **The Smith**: the strength that follows the dream. He raised the mountains
  along her patterns, lit the world's fires, and made the first children:
  the trolls, whom he set aside unfinished, and then the dragons, whom he
  loved and got right.
- **The Gardener**: the patience that follows the strength. She clothed the
  Smith's stone in green, planted the First Tree, and laid out one perfect
  garden as the model from which every wild wood would one day descend.

(Greek in structure: a working pantheon with domains and a family tragedy;
Zelda's golden three in silhouette; the LotR flavor is that they are *makers
who leave*, and the world is what remains of them.)

## 2. The Ages

1. **The Weaving.** The Three make the world southward-to-northward, following
   the Dreamer's dream. The dead sleep. Nothing is wrong yet.
2. **The Gleaming Age.** The first great civilization, **the Gleaming Court**,
   rises across the coasts and lowlands. The Court are mortal, brilliant,
   and long-lived; their craft is *mirror-work*: still pools, polished
   star-glass, monoliths that catch the sky. In a mirror properly ground, a
   Court scryer could glimpse the Dreamer's second world, the Sleeping World,
   taking shape overhead. They built the causeways, the monoliths, the beacon
   towers, and the drowned halls the player now loots. Every place-name with
   *mirror*, *gleam*, *glimmer*, or *shine* in it marks their work: Mirror
   Lake, the Glimmermere, the Mirror Tarn, Bogshine Pools, the Crystalline
   Shallows, the Gleaming Deep.
3. **The Night of Glass.** The Court's hubris (Greek to the bone; the Doom of
   Valyria in execution). Wanting to enter the Sleeping World before it was
   finished, a world with no death in it yet, the Court built their Great
   Mirrors and, on one midsummer night, *pulled* on the Loom's reflection.
   The star cracked. Shards of the Dreamer's heart fell burning across the
   world, the sea rose in her grief, and the whole Gleaming Court drowned in a
   single night. The Dreamer herself fell from the sky and did not rise. And
   because her heart was broken, the dead lost their way into her keeping:
   from that night on, the dead *linger*. Necromancy became possible. Every
   restless corpse in the game is downstream of this one event.
4. **The Long Waking** (now, ~1,200 years later). The Dreamer is not dead:
   she sleeps where she fell, and the world still grows at its rim as she
   dreams on, wounded and slow. At every horizon stands **the Hem**: the
   standing sea-mist where the weave runs out. No ship has ever sailed
   through it; when the Dreamer's dream advances, the Hem recedes somewhere
   and finished land surfaces where it stood. For most of the Long Waking it
   receded northward. Lately, and the Vigil's oldest keepers find this
   either hopeful or terrifying, it has begun thinning to the **east and
   west** instead: the dream has changed direction. But she has begun to *stir*. Rifts tear open
   where her raw dream leaks through. The dead are no longer merely restless
   but gathering. The Gardener, who spent twelve centuries triaging the broken
   world, walked north past her own garden about a hundred years ago and has
   not been seen since. Things are accelerating. Enter the player.

## 3. What each of the Three did on the Night of Glass

These three reactions generate most of the map:

- **The Smith spent himself.** His eldest dragon, **Korzul**, swallowed the
  greatest fallen shard whole, meaning to dream a world of his own with death
  as its only season (Elden Ring's dragon communion, aimed at godhood). The
  Smith fought his firstborn across the Drakelands, the Bloodglass Fields are
  that battle, sand and dragonfire and star-glass fused, dragged him south,
  and bound him beneath a mountain range raised for the purpose: Thornpeak.
  Then, emptied, the Smith lay down *and became the range that holds him*.
  The storms and elementals of Stormcrag are his unquiet strength; Thunzharr,
  who rises there, is a splinter of his wrath that never settled. (Typhon
  under Etna, with the jailer buried alongside the prisoner.)
- **The Gardener triaged.** She froze one autumn forest mid-fall to anchor the
  seasons while the sky healed (the Amberfall: the moment never ends, so the
  leaves never land). She raised a wall against the cold that poured through
  the wound in the sky (the Frostveil's sealed wall). She drew a veil over one
  hidden valley as a seed-vault of the unbroken world (the Veiled Hollow,
  which is why no road reaches it and travel is by portal only). Then, an age
  later, feeling something on the far side of the wound *press back*, she
  walked north through her own garden gate to find her sleeping sister, and
  the Evergarden has tended itself in her absence ever since.
- **The Dreamer fell.** She lies in the caldera the game already calls the
  **Dreamer's Bowl**, in the one realm where night never lifts, the
  Nightbloom, and the half-finished Sleeping World still hangs above her like
  a held breath. (Majora's Mask moon-dread, inverted into melancholy: it isn't
  falling. It's waiting.)

## 4. The surviving institutions (cross-map connective tissue)

Three human-scale orders knit the zones together, all of them decayed the way
the Night's Watch decays: same order, scattered chapters, mostly forgotten
they were ever one thing:

- **The Vigil.** Beacon-keepers. On the Night of Glass, survivors lit a chain
  of flames to call the gods home, and swore to keep them lit until one
  answered. None ever has. Surviving chapters: the **Old Beacon** in the
  Galecrest (the founding flame, it has never once gone out), **Highwatch**
  in Thornpeak (decayed into a plain garrison; Captain Thessaly doesn't know
  what her wall was originally watching *for*), and the **Gullhaven redoubt**
  on the Farshore (the only chapter whose enemy actually showed up, the
  Rifts). The lantern-wardens of Gallowmere in the Wraithwood are a lapsed
  fourth chapter.
- **The Chapel of the Sleep.** The funerary faith founded after the Dreamer
  fell, whose whole liturgy is teaching the dead to sleep *without* her:
  name-ledgers, sexton's bells, the Litany. Its chapters are the game's three
  ruined chapels, the **Fallen Chapel** (Eastbrook Vale), the **Drowned
  Chapel** (Mirefen), the **Mournstone Chapel** (Wraithwood), plus the
  **Collapsed Reliquary** delve (its relic-vault) and the **Drowned Litany**
  delve (its great prayer-hall, lost under the fen). The faith failed slowly
  as the rites weakened. Brother Aldric and Brother Halven are its last honest
  clergy.
- **The Gravecallers** are the Chapel's heresy: not an alien cult but its
  bitter schism. Their founding argument: *if the dead can no longer sleep,
  let them serve.* Their long project: wake Korzul, who holds a shard of the
  Dreamer's heart in his belly, and through him seize the office of Death
  itself. Morthen (Act I), Vael the Mistcaller (Act II), and Grand Necromancer
  Velkhar (Act III) are three rungs of one ladder: this document just gives
  the ladder somewhere to stand.

And one ruin-layer: **Gleaming Court remains** are everywhere their empire
was: the Sunken Bastion, the Sunken Court, the Sunken Idol's temple, the
Leaning Monolith, the Standing Vigil, the Farshore Causeway's drowned
stonework, and every mirror-pool listed above.

# The Realms

## Eastbrook Vale: *the Quiet Inheritance*

> "Bury them with their names and a bell, and check the bell for a week."
> Vale custom, older than anyone can say why

The Vale is what the world looks like when the catastrophe is twelve centuries
stale: farmland over ruins, festivals over funerals. Its people are
descendants of the Gleaming Court's *servants*, the grooms, orchard-hands,
and masons who lived inland of the wave, which is why Vale families keep odd
heirloom customs (mirror-cloths over standing water at a wake) without
remembering their reason. Mirror Lake is a Court scrying pool gone wild;
the Farshore Causeway is Court stonework; Reliquary Hill was the Chapel of the
Sleep's vault of quiet relics, and the Fallen Chapel was its mother-church,
where Sexton Marrow rang the bell for the buried until Morthen made the bell
answer back. The Sowfield's boarball cup is an old harvest rite with the
religion long since laundered out of it: the "goal" was once a grave.

**Threads:** Morthen came here *because* of the Fallen Chapel's well-kept
name-ledgers: a necromancer's shopping list. Mirror Lake ↔ the Court's
mirror-craft ↔ the Night of Glass. The Causeway leads to the Farshore chapter
of the Vigil.

## Mirefen Marsh: *the Sea That Never Left*

> "The fen isn't rising, traveler. It never finished falling."

Before the Night of Glass this was the Gleaming Court's richest lowland
province. The wave that drowned the Court never fully receded here: the
marsh *is* the flood, gone green and patient. The Sunken Bastion is a Court
fortress; the Drowned Litany was the Chapel of the Sleep's greatest
prayer-hall, built after the flood to sing the drowned to sleep, and lost to
the same waters it prayed against. The Deepfen crater is a shard-wound from
the falling Loom. The trolls, the Smith's discarded first-made, older than
the Court and still sore about the dragons, dug their mounds into burial
grounds that predate everything, and woke what the Chapel had barely managed
to lull. Vael the Mistcaller's genius was simple: the fen is full of drowned
Court dead *who were never taught to sleep at all*.

**Threads:** the Drowned Dead are Gleaming Court subjects: Act II is looting
the Night of Glass directly. Brother Aldric's wall-relic on the east rim is
Chapel masonry. The trolls recur at the Trollmoot in the Drakelands: one
sundered elder race, two continents.

## Thornpeak Heights: *the Jailer's Body*

> "The mountain doesn't shake because something is trying to get out.
> It shakes because something is still holding on."

Thornpeak is not a natural range. The Smith raised it in one night to bind
Korzul, then lay down along its spine and became it. Every quake is the
prisoner turning; every elemental shaken loose at Stormcrag is the jailer's
strength fraying loose of his purpose; the Glimmermere is the lake that
gathered in the crater where Korzul's swallowed shard first struck earth:
the Wyrmcult camps beside it because the water still *shows things*. Korgath
the Bound was the Smith's foreman, a giant set as living lock on the seal;
the cult's cruelest work was binding the binder. Highwatch is a Vigil chapter
that forgot itself: Thessaly's order has watched this wall for forty
generations without remembering they were posted here to watch the *mountain*.

**Threads:** Korzul's shard is one of the fallen pieces of the Dreamer's
heart: the Act III finale is, unknowingly, a fight over a fragment of a
goddess. Thunzharr = the Smith's splintered wrath. The ogres selling their
axes to the cult echoes the Court's original sin: mortals renting out the
world's bones for a shortcut.

## The Farshore: *Where the Sky Is Thin*

> "The bell means come. The horn means run. You'll learn which is which
> faster than you'd think."

The shards of the Loom passed low over this island on their way down, and the
sky here never healed: it scars, and the scars split. The Riftfields are
where the Dreamer's raw, unfinished dream leaks through: this is the lore of
Rift dungeons themselves. **No two Rifts are ever alike because the dream
never repeats**; a Rift is a room of the Sleeping World that was never
finished, briefly pressed against ours. Gullhaven's redoubt is the one Vigil
chapter whose vigil was ever *answered*: just not by a god. They ring the
bell, hold the shore, and have stopped writing reports nobody inland believes.

**Threads:** Rifts, mechanically procedural, are narratively *dream-stuff*:
even the AI-upgraded ones slot in ("the dream was more vivid tonight").
The Causeway ties it to the Vale; the redoubt's founding charter names the
Old Beacon in the Galecrest as its mother-house, though neither remembers the
other exists.

## The Veiled Hollow: *the Seed Vault*

> "Nothing in the Hollow is the last of its kind. That is the entire point
> of the Hollow." (Keeper Saelwyn)

When the sea rose, the Gardener saved exactly one valley: not the Court's
lords, but its gardeners, orchard-keepers, and singers, folk who had tended
rather than taken. She drew a veil woven from dream-light over the whole
vale, cut every road, and planted a cutting of the First Tree at its heart:
Eldergleam, under which Keeper Saelwyn's line has kept the veil for sixty
generations (their long lives are the tree's doing). Starfall Basin holds the
shard whose light the veil is woven from: the Hollow's protection and its
single point of failure. The Sunken Court on its coast is the one Court city
the veil half-saved: drowned to the rooftops, but *gently*, its dead among
the very few who sleep sound. The Gleaming Deep and Crystalline Shallows
gleam with star-glass sifted down from the Basin.

**Threads:** portal-only access is the veil, not geography. Eldergleam ↔ the
Gardener ↔ the Evergarden. If the Gravecallers ever learned a heart-shard
sits in Starfall Basin, undefended by anything but a curtain of light:
that's an expansion.

## The Drakelands: *the Forge and the Civil War*

> "Every dragon you see flying is one that said no."

The Smith's forge-country: the Drakemaw Caldera is the forge itself, still
lit; the Cinder Dunes are twelve centuries of its ash. This is where the
dragons were made, and where they broke. When Korzul swallowed the shard, he
offered his kin the same communion, *dream your own worlds, be gods of your
own seasons*, and the flights sundered. The loyal fought him here: the
Bloodglass Fields are dune-sand fused by dragonfire into acres of dark glass,
with both armies still in it. The drakes wheeling over Wyrmwatch today are
the loyalists' diminished descendants, keeping a vigil over the *south*, over
Thornpeak, waiting for the day the mountain fails. The Trollmoot burns with
the Smith's firstborn, who squat in their maker's country out of spite and
grief in equal measure.

**Threads:** Wyrmwatch and Highwatch are two ends of one watch, dragon and
human, neither aware of the other. Bloodglass = Night of Glass, localized.
The trolls bridge to the Mirefen mounds.

## The Frostveil Reach: *the Wall and the Other*

> "The lights are beautiful. Keep walking."

The Loom's fall tore a wound in the sky, and through it leaked the cold
*between* worlds: not winter, but the outer dark given weather (the
primordial, pre-creation cold; Chaos with a wind-chill). The Gardener and the
last Court masons raised the sealed wall on the south rim to pen it onto this
one massif. The Aurora Steps' dancing lights are the veil between worlds
visibly fraying: beautiful and wrong. And the cold "feels awake" because it
is: a shard fell into the deep ice under Glacier Tarn, and something on the
far side of the wound has been *dreaming into it*: a dream that is not the
Dreamer's. The ice-fishers of Icemantle carve little idols of what they see
in bad winters. Travelers from the Palmreach go pale when shown one.

**Threads:** the Other is the world's long-game villain seed: the reason the
Gardener finally walked north. Its idols match the Sunken Idol in the
Palmreach, which predates creation's coastline. The wall ↔ GoT; the awake
cold ↔ the Frostveil welcome text, now load-bearing.

## The Amberfall: *the Held Instant*

> "Autumn is when things let go. We are the moment before."

On the Night of Glass the seasons themselves began to unravel, so the
Gardener chose one autumn weald at the moment of perfect turning and *held
it*. Every leaf in the Amberfall has been mid-fall for twelve hundred years;
none has ever landed. The realm is one continuous held breath: which is why
it feels so golden and so faintly grief-struck at once (this is the zone's
Elden Ring note: a suspended golden age you walk through, knowing it's a
wound). The Great Mere was the Court's grandest mirror; Lanternmere's folk
light lanterns every dusk in a tribute whose origin they've forgotten: they
are relighting the Loom. The Leaning Monolith is Court work, knocked atilt by
the star's passage; sight along its lean and it points, exactly, at the
Nightbloom.

**Threads:** if the leaves ever fall, the last hour of the old world finishes
happening: nobody knows what was queued behind it. The Golden Shelf and the
gold road tie its trade south. The Monolith is a literal signpost to the
Dreamer's resting place.

## The Willowfen: *the Spilled Lullaby*

> "People sleep well in Bridgemere. That's the whole of it, and it's more
> than it sounds."

When the Dreamer fell, her sleep spilled, and it pooled in the lowest, widest
basin of the north. The Willowfen's drowsy gentleness is not weather: it is
residue of a goddess's rest. The hum of its bees and dragonflies carries the
last audible scrap of the lullaby she once sang the dead, which is why this
is the *only* realm where the dead still sleep sound on their own. Families
from three realms carry their dead to Willowweep's willow-isle for burial,
and always have, without being able to say why it works. The Braids, the
east water-meadows, dissolve into channels that old fenwives say rearrange
themselves to match whatever the Dreamer is dreaming that season.

**Threads:** the gentle zone is gentle *for a reason* that is one hop from
the game's darkest lore. Gravecaller doctrine has a special hatred for the
Willowfen: soil where nothing they plant will rise. The Amberfen Steps link
it to the held autumn next door.

## The Nightbloom: *Where She Lies*

> "Past the Nightgate, mind your dreams. They may not start out as yours."

The Dreamer fell here, and the night fell with her and never lifted: not
darkness, but *her* night, soft and floral and lit from below by blooms that
open for a moon that isn't there. She lies in the Dreamer's Bowl, and
directly above the caldera hangs the Sleeping World: the second world she was
weaving, half-finished, moored in the sky like a ship waiting on its captain
(the game's existing "sleeping world that hangs in the sky"). The Moonwell is
pooled dream-light; drink and you dream true, which the locals of Moonrest
treat with the same practical caution as strong drink. The Standing Vigil are
Gleaming Court penitents who walked here after the Drowning and turned to
stone waiting for her to wake: the politest fate any Court survivor got. The
Sleepless Barrow is the realm's one wrongness: a barrow whose dead will not
sleep *even here*. No one has yet asked what they were buried with.

**Threads:** the realm the Leaning Monolith points at; the source of the
Willowfen's spilled sleep; the Rifts' dream leaks through thinnest here. The
Nightgate keeps sleepwalkers *in*, not enemies out. Endgame gravity: whatever
finally stirs her: Korzul's waking, the Other's pressing, the player:
happens over this bowl.

## The Wraithwood: *the Forest of Lost Names*

> "The wood repeats every name it has ever heard, hoping one of you will
> answer and stay."

When the dead lost their road into the dream, the unclaimed ones drifted
north on the wind like leaves, and snagged. The Wraithwood grew tall on
twelve centuries of lost souls caught in its canopies; it is less haunted
than *inhabited*, a forest-shaped waiting room. The Huntsman was a lord of
the Gleaming Court who survived the Drowning and swore to gather his drowned
people's souls home; he hunts still, long past being alive himself, and his
Wild Hunt through the Hanging Glade is the closest thing the lost dead have
to a shepherd (Erlking by way of the Stranger). Gallowmere's lantern-line is
a lapsed Vigil chapter that now guides souls instead of gods. The Mournstone
Chapel is the Chapel of the Sleep's last northern chapter, where one brother
still rings a bell the dead can follow: the wood's whispering is every name
from every ledger, read back.

**Threads:** the zone-level dramatization of the world's core wound (dead
who can't sleep) at its gentlest and eeriest. The Crowgate faces the
Nightbloom: the wood's dead press toward her and cannot enter her night.
The Huntsman knew the Court's drowned names: including, perhaps, the Three's
true names. A loremaster's grail.

## The Palmreach: *the First Fire and the Older Thing*

> "The idol was on the seafloor before there was a sea. The Court only
> built the temple."

The Firemount is the Smith's *first* forge: where he learned fire before he
built the Drakemaw, which is why the volcano's temper is clumsy, young-gods
work. The jungle is the Gardener's one unpruned draft: she never came back to
thin it, so it grows without rule, gorgeous and lawless (the Emerald Tangle,
the Vinefall). The Court kept a colony here whose divers brought up something
from the deep that should not have predated the world, and built the Sunken
Idol's temple around it; the Drowning took the colony, and locals say the
temple sank *relieved*. Sea-glass from drowned Court windows still washes
into the Sapphire Lagoon, and Drifthaven's children wear an empire's windows
as beads.

**Threads:** the Idol matches the Frostveil ice-carvings: the Other has been
worshipped at both ends of the world's temperature. The Firemount ↔ Drakemaw
gives the Smith a career arc you can walk. Tropical zone, but its deep lore
is the coldest thread in the game.

## The Evergarden: *the Unattended Masterpiece*

> "Someone is still trimming the hedges. Nobody is paid to. Mind the maze:
> it minds you back."

The Gardener's own seat: the model garden from which all wild green
descends. For eleven hundred years after the Night of Glass she stayed here,
diminishing the way makers do when their siblings are gone (the LotR fading,
in one figure), tending roses while the world rationed itself around her
triage. A hundred years ago she felt two things in one night: her sister
*stir*, and the Other *press*. She walked out through the Garden Gate, north,
and did not lock it behind her: she locked the Great Maze instead. The
garden has tended itself since: hedges trimmed, fountains fed from no spring,
the Statuary Walk one statue longer each decade (petitioners who waited too
long for an audience: look closely, some are mid-bow). Hedgewick's families
have served the garden for generations without once seeing their employer,
and consider this proper.

**Threads:** the Great Maze is the lock on the path she took: solve it and
you follow a god's footprints toward wherever the story goes next (the
built-in door for future northward realms). Eldergleam in the Hollow is a
cutting from her First Tree. The Rose Wilds going feral at the edges is the
world's quietest countdown: the garden is very slowly forgetting her.

## The Galecrest: *the Held Breath and the Kept Promise*

> "The wind hasn't stopped in twelve hundred years, and the Beacon hasn't
> gone out. Around here we call that a fair bargain."

When the Loom fell, it left a hole in the heavens, and the sky has been
rushing to fill it ever since: the Galecrest's wind is the world's held
breath escaping, and it will not stop until the wound closes. On the Night of
Glass itself, the first Vigilants climbed these headlands and lit the Old
Beacon to call the Three home, swearing it would burn until one answered.
Twelve centuries of Wickharbor keepers have kept the vow; the order has
forgotten nearly everything else about itself, but not this. The Wreckfields
are every ship that mistook the promise for a lighthouse. And in the middle
of the endless wind lies the Mirror Tarn, a Court scrying pool the gale
refuses to touch: dead calm, always: because its surface still shows the sky
as it was *before* the Night of Glass. Keepers go there once a year, on the
anniversary, to look at the star.

**Threads:** mother-house of the Vigil: Highwatch, Gullhaven, and Gallowmere
are its forgotten daughters. The Mirror Tarn is the only place in the world
you can see the Loom whole: the single most valuable scrying surface alive,
which the Gravecallers, the Wyrmcult, and worse would all kill for. The
Shear's cliffs face the Farshore's torn sky across the water.

## 5. How the existing campaign sits inside this

- **Act I (Vale):** Morthen isn't robbing a random chapel: he's harvesting
  the Chapel of the Sleep's best-kept ledgers, in the faith's mother-church.
- **Act II (Mirefen):** Vael's Drowned Dead are Gleaming Court subjects; the
  players are fighting the Night of Glass's leftovers, raised by the Chapel's
  own heretics.
- **Act III (Thornpeak):** Korzul holds a shard of the Dreamer's heart. The
  Gravecallers' endgame is not "wake a dragon" but "seize the office of
  Death." Killing Korzul at 20 quietly leaves the question the whole world
  now asks: *what happens to the shard?* (Answer: whatever the next
  expansion wants.)
- **Rifts:** unfinished rooms of the Sleeping World pressing through the torn
  sky. Procedural = the dream never repeats. First-clear races = the leak
  seals when someone finishes the dream properly.
- **World growth at the rim:** the Dreamer dreams on, slow and wounded. New
  realms surfacing wherever the Hem recedes is *canon*, not retcon: the
  world is literally unfinished, and every new zone shipped is her dream
  advancing. The long northward run is history; the Hem is thinning east and
  west now, so new maps landing on either flank need no explanation at all.
  (The Galecrest, the world's first east-column realm, is remembered as the
  first sign the dream had turned.)

## 6. Inspiration ledger

- **Greek myth:** the family-tragedy pantheon; hubris → drowning (the Court);
  the titan bound under the mountain with the jailer become the mountain
  (Typhon/Etna); sleep and death as siblings (Hypnos/Thanatos) fused into the
  Dreamer's office.
- **Elden Ring:** the shattered celestial order with nameless, titled gods;
  the golden suspended moment (Amberfall); dragon communion on a god-shard
  (Korzul); a precursor civilization you meet only as ruins and consequences.
- **Game of Thrones:** the Wall and the decayed watch order (Frostveil, the
  Vigil); the Doom-of-Valyria single-night cataclysm; institutions that
  outlive their own memory.
- **Lord of the Rings:** the maker-gods who fade and leave (the Gardener);
  the hidden valley refuge (Veiled Hollow ↔ Rivendell); the beacon-chain;
  the long defeat held off by small keepers of small flames.
- **Legend of Zelda:** the triune makers; the ominous world hanging in the
  sky (Nightbloom ↔ Majora); the lost wood that keeps your name (Wraithwood);
  a sleeping power that ages wait on.
- **World of Warcraft:** sundered dragonflights keeping vigil (Drakelands);
  elemental unrest as a symptom of a buried titan; death cults with
  infrastructure; rifts and portals as endgame texture.
- **Dark Souls:** the kept flame in a fading age (the Old Beacon: keepers
  feeding a fire whose promise nobody living remembers, and the quiet dread
  of what happens if it dies); the undead curse as worldwide condition rather
  than local monster problem (the dead who cannot find the Sleep); tragic
  jailers who became their own prisons (the Smith as Thornpeak, Korgath the
  Bound); melancholy ruins that were somebody's golden age (every Court
  site); the Statuary Walk's petitioners, hollowed mid-bow.
- **Warhammer:** the Other as the Chaos-beyond-the-veil: a hostile
  intelligence pressing through a wound in reality, with Rifts as its
  incursion mechanic and matching cult-idols at both poles; corruption that
  works through schism and doctrine rather than armies (the Gravecallers as
  a cult *inside* the Chapel of the Sleep); the sealed Frostveil wall as the
  grim northern front held by too few; ogres and trolls as mercenary muscle
  bought by whoever pays (Drogmar's clans, the Trollmoot).

## 7. Open hooks (deliberately unresolved)

1. **The Other**: what dreams back through the wound; idols at both poles.
2. **The Gardener's trail**: the Great Maze opens onto wherever she went;
   natural frame for the next continent-scale expansion.
3. **The Sleepless Barrow**: dead that even the Nightbloom cannot soothe.
4. **The Huntsman's ledger**: the drowned true names of the Three.
5. **Korzul's shard**: a piece of a goddess's heart, ownerless at level 20.
6. **The falling leaves**: the trigger nobody wants to test in the Amberfall.
7. **The Mirror Tarn**: the last image of the unbroken sky, and everyone who
   wants it.

# 8. The Undreamed Lands: lore store for future maps

The cosmology makes new maps canon by construction: the Dreamer is still
dreaming the world northward, so every realm we ship is simply land that has
*finished surfacing*. This section is the standing stockpile: realm concepts
already wired into the existing threads, ready to pull from whenever a new
map is needed. None of them is dated or promised; they are seeds, not specs.

## 8.0 The two new edges

The Hem is thinning east and west, and the two flanks surface with different
tempers, use these as the flavor baseline for whichever side a new map
lands on:

- **East, the Morrowlands** (toward the dawn). The Loom rose in the east,
  so the eastern weave is the *freshest* dream: brighter, stranger, less
  settled: coasts, archipelagos, glass, and morning light. Eastern realms
  skew wondrous-with-teeth. The Galecrest and the Farshore are the existing
  eastern column; new east maps extend their line. (Zelda's wonder, early
  Elden Ring's golden strangeness.)
- **West: the Evenlands** (toward the dusk). The sun goes west, and the old
  funeral rites followed it: west was the direction of the honored dead
  even before the Night of Glass. The western weave is *older* dream,
  surfacing out of order: grey light, long shadows, memory standing around
  in fields. Western realms skew elegiac and haunted. (Dark Souls' fading
  kingdoms, the Wraithwood's register.)

Rule of thumb: the east asks "what is this new thing?", the west asks "whose
was this?", and the north road stays what it always was: the Gardener's
trail toward the Dreamer and the Other.

## 8.1 Realm concepts, ready to grow

Each concept below carries a suggested compass fit, but none is locked to it.

### The Thornway: *the Gardener's Trail* (temperate bramble-wilds · north)
The land beyond the Great Maze, reachable only by solving it. A hundred years
of the Gardener's footprints: hedgerows grown over her campsites, a rose
blooming in the dead of winter wherever she slept, and her pruning-hook left
hanging on a gate no wall was ever built around. The realm's wrongness: her
trail gets *younger* as you go north: as if she is still just ahead.
**Pulls threads:** Evergarden (the Maze), Veiled Hollow (Eldergleam feels her
pass), the Other (what she walked north to face).

### The Tidecourt: *the Drowned Capital* (shallow-sea ruin realm · west)
The Gleaming Court's capital, drowned intact on the Night of Glass, its
towers now a reef between low tide and memory. Streets walkable at ebb,
swimmable at flood; the Great Mirrors still stand in the throne-plaza,
face-down. The drowned here are not hostile by default: they hold court,
twelve centuries into a session that never adjourned, and attack only those
who track mud on the mosaics. Somewhere below sits the Mirror That Pulled,
cracked from rim to rim. **Pulls threads:** the Night of Glass origin site;
the Huntsman's people; the true names of the Three, spoken nowhere else.

### The Cindershelf: *the Smith's Workshop* (basalt flows and dead forges · west)
Between the Drakelands and the western Hem: the Smith's proving-grounds,
littered with his rejected drafts: half-made mountains, a river he never
lit, anvils the size of villages. The trolls make pilgrimage here to argue
with their maker's leavings. The wrongness: one forge, deepest in, is still
warm and *tidy*, its tools recently cleaned, but the Smith lies as Thornpeak,
so who keeps the shop? **Pulls threads:** trolls (Mirefen, Trollmoot),
Drakemaw and Firemount (the forge lineage), Thunzharr (splinters of wrath).

### The Hushwold: *where the wind stops* (still, snowless white forest · east)
East past the Galecrest's gale: a realm of total calm: the one
place the world's escaping breath does not reach, because something on the
other side of the wound is *inhaling*. Sound behaves wrongly; bells ring a
day late. The Other's front porch, and the Gardener's last confirmed
sighting. Frostveil's wall wardens speak of it only as "the quiet."
**Pulls threads:** the Other (idols, the awake cold), the Galecrest (wind
lore inverted), the Gardener's trail terminus candidate.

### The Sleepless Road: *the old way of the dead* (grey twilight marchland · west)
Before the Night of Glass, the dead walked a road into the Dreamer's keeping,
and the road ran west, after the sun: which is why the west was the
direction of funerals long before anyone remembered why.
The road is still there, a grey causeway across a colorless heath, but it
now ends at a broken gate, and twelve centuries of arrivals wait in queue,
patient as weather. The Wraithwood's lost souls drift here eventually; the
Huntsman drives his gathered dead up this road and has never once gotten one
through the gate. A realm about grief with almost no combat pressure: the
Willowfen's dark mirror. **Pulls threads:** the whole death cosmology;
Wraithwood; the Gravecallers (who would pay anything to hold the gate).

### The Glasswold: *where a shard fell on sand* (crystal desert · east)
An eastern-sea desert isle where a Loom-shard struck dunes: the impact fused
a hundred miles of sand into a rolling country of smoked glass. By day it is
blinding; by night the buried shard-light rises through the glass like a slow
dawn underfoot. Court prospectors mined it once: their cutting-camps and
one intact Great Lens remain. Scrying anywhere in the Glasswold works too
well: you see what you looked for, plus one thing you did not.
**Pulls threads:** shard-sites (Starfall Basin, Glimmermere), Court
mirror-craft, a shard the Gravecallers could actually reach.

### The Wavebreak Isles: *the scattered fleet* (storm archipelago · east)
The Court's navy put to sea on the Night of Glass and was thrown across a
hundred islands, each now home to descendants of one ship: an archipelago of
tiny cultures, one per crew, some noble, some gone strange (the Odyssey as a
zone map: every island one episode). The wrecks' figureheads are carved
with faces that locals swear change expression before storms.
**Pulls threads:** the Court diaspora, Galecrest's Wreckfields, Palmreach
sea-glass; a natural sailing/naval-content realm.

### The Halfdream: *the Sleeping World itself* (endgame sky realm · above)
The far end of the story: setting foot inside the Dreamer's unfinished second
world, via the Nightbloom or a stabilized Rift. Geography obeys dream-rules:
districts unfinished mid-thought, a horizon with visible weave, rooms the
player has seen before in Rifts now standing in their true positions. There
is no death here yet, which is exactly why everyone from Korzul's shard-heirs
to the Other wants to move in. **Pulls threads:** everything: Rifts, the
Nightbloom, the Loom, the endgame question of who inherits a deathless world.

## 8.2 The realm-minting recipe

Any new map, including ones invented long after this document, stays canon
if it answers six questions:

1. **Which Age marked it?** Weaving-era god-work, a Gleaming Age province or
   colony, a Night-of-Glass scar, or newly surfaced Long-Waking land.
2. **Which of the Three touched it?** One fingerprint minimum: the Smith's
   stone and fire, the Gardener's green and order, or the Dreamer's sleep,
   night, and dream-logic. (Newly surfaced land = the Dreamer by default.)
3. **Which order had a chapter here?** A Vigil flame, a Chapel of the Sleep
   chapter-house, or pointedly *neither*: absence is also a story.
4. **Where does it gleam?** One mirror/gleam/glimmer-named water or glass
   site per realm: Court ruin or shard-light. This is the setting's
   signature motif; keep the streak alive.
5. **What is its one wrongness?** Each realm carries exactly one impossible,
   quietly dreadful fact (leaves that never land, hedges that trim
   themselves, wind that never stops). One is haunting; three is noise.
6. **Which two existing realms does it touch?** Every new map connects to at
   least two shipped ones through history, an order, a bloodline, or a
   pointing monument: never geography alone.
