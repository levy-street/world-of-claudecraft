Monster Harvesting (single-use corpses) and focus

The deliberate opposite of world nodes, to make specialization social and to justify higher-value materials.

  • Each corpse can be gathered once, first come. Corpses are a shared, scarce resource.
  • Scarcity rewards coordination: a regular group plans who specializes in which monster component types, so each member knows which monsters to harvest and which to leave for a teammate.
  • A monster is tagged with one or more component types (horn, hide, venom sac, gills, ...), defined per mob as content.
  • At the corpse, the UI lets the player focus on a single component type or several at once. Fewer components yields higher tier; spreading across many yields lower tier each.

Focus: removed

The persistent, town-set additive focus allocation (and its time, coin or material re-spec) shipped as Town Focus and was removed from the game on 2026-11-04: its launcher only appeared in town, so nobody found it, and the mechanic was judged not useful. The per-corpse choice above is the whole of the harvesting tradeoff now; every component yields its baseline tier and quantity with no allocation on top.

Signed materials and the self-gathered bonus

  • Gathering a rare or better monster material stamps the item with the gatherer's name (a signed material).
  • Using a material you gathered yourself in a craft grants a bonus: either a small lift to the output's item-level budget, or a reduction in the quantity of that material the recipe requires.
  • This rewards the player who does both halves, and turns a signed material from someone else into a mark of provenance rather than a transferable mechanical bonus.

Why account-level breadth does not break exclusivity

An account can hold many characters, so a dedicated player can in principle spread the archetypes across alts and cover the whole wheel over time. That does not collapse the design, because the top of crafting is gated behind adventuring, not just crafting. The best materials come from Monster Harvesting, which means killing higher-tier monsters, and later Battlefield Experience (committed concept, later phase; see Part II: Future) improves what you craft. Both require a character that actually fights, so a combat level 1 crafting alt is never prevented from crafting but is capped well below an adventurer-crafter. Monster Harvesting alone carries this gate today; Battlefield Experience only deepens it. This is the pillar that negates the one-alt-per-craft pattern that guts crafting value in so many MMOs.

Two further inputs reinforce it. Contested single-use corpses are an always-on friction no one can multibox. And a recipe that requires a material signed by its recipient removes the shared-gatherer-alt shortcut for bound gear, because the wearer has to have gathered it themselves. A bare signature on its own is only provenance, not an anti-alt lever (each alt signs its own materials); the exclusivity comes from the adventuring gate and the contested and recipient-bound inputs, not the signature. You can only ever play one or two characters at once, so you can never bring the top of every archetype to bear at the same time. Specialization stays per-character, and a fully rewarding pure-skiller route is an explicit non-goal for now.
