World of ClaudeCraft · The Farshore · Riftspawn, round two

Horns, purple, extra limbs Five more Riftspawn, built from what you actually liked.

The mob is locked: the Riftspawn. First quest on the island, Q0 "Ashore": report to Sergeant Marsh at the Watch Meadow, then kill twelve of these on the militia line. First enemy anyone fights in the campaign. Level 3 to 4, entity colour violet 0x7a3fb0, a little under two yards tall.

The brief for this round: horns, saturated purple, extra limbs. No architecture, no hoods, no cloaks, nothing tailored. Bare bodies, so the horns and the limbs carry the silhouette.

Two things I fixed from the first round, both my errors. I had asked for "dark desaturated violet-grey" and got five nearly black bodies; this round asks for saturated purple with deep purple shadows instead of black, which is why these actually read as purple. And the word "drape" reliably produced clothing, so this round says bare, twice. The architecture in round one came out of a design doc in the repo, not from the brief, and it is gone.

On extra limbs being expensive: mostly they are not. Extra arms only cost real money if they need to move independently. A second pair bound rigidly to the chest bone, folded or clasped, rides the torso through every animation for free: no new bones, no per-clip posing, all 22 KayKit clips work untouched. That is exactly the pose the strongest candidate below already has. Only free-swinging extra arms need paying for.

The Four-Arm Imp

All three likes, at once

My pick

4,073 tris · 1 material

  • Horns
  • Purple
  • Four arms
  • Free to animate

What works

Big curling ram horns with magenta along the ridges, huge cold glowing eyes, and the second pair of arms clasped tight against its chest, which is precisely the free-to-animate configuration. Digitigrade legs ending in black claws. Of everything generated across both rounds this is the one that could stand next to the KayKit cast today with no adjustment: the proportions, the flat colour blocks and the chunky facets are all native. It is also the cheapest to finish.

What is wrong with it

It is cute. Big eyes on a small body reads as adorable more than nightmarish, and this thing is supposed to be what a rift vomits onto a besieged island. Whether that matters is a taste call and yours to make: plenty of classic MMOs make their level 3 trash appealing on purpose. If you want it meaner, the fix is small and local: shrink the eyes, drop the brow, and lengthen the jaw.

The Antler Stalker

No mouth

If you want eerie

3,961 tris · 1 material

  • Horns
  • Purple
  • Four arms

What works

The eeriest thing either round produced, and the reason is one detail: it has no mouth. A narrow blank face, two cold eyes, and nothing else. It does not eat and it does not speak, which is a far stranger idea than a set of teeth. The antlers branch wider than its own body, so its silhouette is the most distinctive here, and four long thin arms with oversized clawed hands finish it.

What is wrong with it

The thin limbs and narrow torso are the least chibi proportions of the batch, so it sits slightly outside the KayKit family; it would want thickening. The antlers are wide enough to clip through doorways and tight terrain, which is a real problem for a mob that spawns in numbers. And its arms are held out rather than folded, so if all four need to swing it costs animation work the imp does not.

The Mantis

Not a person at all

Third

3,941 tris · 1 material

  • Horns
  • Purple
  • Four arms
  • Free to animate

What works

Swept antenna horns, hexagonal chitin plates on the shoulders, and two pairs of clawed hands folded up against the chest, again in the free configuration. The most genuinely alien of the five: it is not a small person with horns, it is something with the wrong number of everything. The purple carapace reads well and the eyes are the right cold blue.

What is wrong with it

It reads insect before it reads demon, which pulls against the mob's declared demon family and against the lore, where these things come out of a dreaming world rather than a hive. The antennae are thin enough to disappear at gameplay distance, taking the horn read with them.

The Horn Thicket

Horns as the whole design

Wrong tier

3,770 tris · 1 material

  • Horns
  • Purple
  • No extra limbs
  • Eyes came out green

What works

If horns are the thing you like, this is the horn design: a domed skull studded with uneven spikes radiating in every direction, with the face sunk underneath them. Long heavy arms hanging to the ground and a hunched stance give it genuine weight, and it is the only one of the ten that looks like it could actually hurt the militia line.

What is wrong with it

It has no extra limbs, and the eyes came out sickly green instead of the cold blue every other break-spawned thing burns, so it breaks the family mark. Mostly though it is the wrong tier: this reads as a boss or a rare, not as the thing you kill twelve of in the opening quest. Worth keeping for later.

The Six-Arm

Most limbs, least threat

Weakest

3,619 tris · 1 material

  • Horns
  • Purple
  • Six arms
  • Ignored the "no legs" instruction

What works

Six arms fanned down its sides is the boldest limb statement of the batch, and it reads clearly from every angle. If "multiple limbs" is the idea you care most about, this is the purest expression of it.

What is wrong with it

I asked for no legs at all, with the lower body tapering to a wisp of smoke so it floated. The generator ignored that completely and gave it ordinary legs, so the idea that made it interesting is missing. What is left is a cat-faced imp with a lot of arms, and it is the cutest of the ten by a distance, which for this mob is the wrong direction.

Finishing is free, and it is also the better result

The obvious next step would be Tripo's own rig plus animation retarget, about $1.05 for a small preset clip set. There is a better option that costs nothing. The pipeline ships a rig-manual lane that fits a generated mesh onto the actual KayKit skeleton locally and computes the skin weights on this machine. The model then inherits the reference body's entire clip library natively: all 22 KayKit animations, including the Block and strafe clips Tripo's preset library cannot supply at any price.

That matters for style, not just money. Every figure already in the concept book moves on those exact clips, so the Riftspawn would animate identically to Ewald and Marsh rather than merely looking similar while moving differently. Both requirements are met by all five candidates: they are T-posed, and they are humanoid enough in proportion.

One thing worth saying about picking

These are not mutually exclusive. The campaign needs several break-spawned bodies, and the review notes are explicit that its two commonest enemies must not share a silhouette. The imp and the antler stalker are about as far apart as two purple horned things can be, so shipping the imp as the Riftspawn and the stalker as its sibling the Breach Wretch would solve two problems with one batch. The horn thicket would then sit nicely as something rarer and heavier.

Pick one and I will

  • Rig it onto the KayKit skeleton with all 22 clips, free, then render every clip and check for weight bleed at the joints.
  • Take it into Blender for whatever the notes above call for: thickening the stalker's limbs, or shrinking the imp's eyes and lengthening its jaw if you want it meaner.
  • Retune the albedo so it sits close to the violet entity colour, so the game's runtime tint reinforces the art instead of washing it out. That mistake is why the shipped wolf's tint is invisible in every frame.
  • Wire it in: the VisualDef, the MOB_KEYS entry, the real world height, then a screenshot of it standing on the militia line in the running game.
  • Add it to the concept book with a turntable and playable clips, like the two figures already published there.

If none of these is right either, another five is $2.75 and about twelve minutes. Naming the parts you like from across the ten is the fastest way to get there; that is what turned round one into round two.

Round one, rejected

Kept for reference

$2.75 · five candidates

These leaned on "architectural fragments as body parts", which came from docs/design/last-bell-cast-review-notes.md section 4.4, not from the brief. Three of the five were also hooded shrouds, and the palette instruction I wrote produced near-black bodies rather than purple. Recorded here so the reasoning is not lost, and because the sleeper's four folded arms are what led to this round.

Provenance. Concept images and meshes generated through scripts/asset_pipeline/pipeline.mjs creature (Tripo API), each stopped at --until generate so no rigging was paid for. Model plates rendered in Blender through scripts/assets/last_bell_crew/preview.py, the same lighting rig as the published concept book, framed on each mesh's true bounding box. Triangle and material counts measured at import. Front is yaw 90, measured rather than assumed: Tripo's raw output faces a different axis than the book's rig expects.
